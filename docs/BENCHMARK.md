# Benchmark contract v0.2.0

## Scope

This version benchmarks **multi-terminal arrival metering**, not full air traffic control. Airport coordinates are approximate, manually curated map labels. Each airport is an independent 2D terminal sector, with one synthetic runway. Real runway counts, geography, routes, terrain and airport elevations are not modeled. A straight-line descent is an abstraction, not a cleared real-world approach.

Aircraft are generated by a seeded PRNG. Exogenous arrivals and weather are independent of the agent's actions. Holding fixes are arranged in separated rings. Holding motion is a small circular pattern sampled in one-second linear segments. Airborne fuel is a countdown. On clearance, aircraft move toward the runway at commanded speed times the weather factor; altitude descends linearly with remaining distance. Clearances are not automatically made safe by the environment.

There are no heading/altitude controls, departures, diversions, emergency maneuvers, go-arounds, real flight plans, wake categories or inter-sector interactions in this initial version. Extension proposals are welcome under a new version. Observation-based automation can coordinate multiple airports, but cannot claim validated real-world ATC capability from this benchmark.

## Safety and scoring

An aircraft earns **one safe landing** if it reaches an open, available runway, has fuel remaining, and has never been involved in a separation loss. Aircraft that collided or exhausted fuel cannot land. A landing consumes the runway occupancy interval; touchdown during that interval or a closure is a runway incident. Unsafe clearances are executed and recorded, not silently corrected.

A separation loss occurs when both horizontal distance is below 3 NM and vertical separation is below 1,000 ft. A collision uses 0.12 NM and 100 ft. These are synthetic benchmark parameters, not a complete implementation of aviation regulations. Real separation depends on context; see the [FAA AIM discussion of aircraft separation](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/aim_html/chap4_section_4.html).

For each one-second movement segment, the engine solves intersection intervals for horizontal distance and vertical altitude and tests whether those intervals overlap. This catches crossing conflicts between sample endpoints. Comparisons are within the same terminal sector; different airports are independent. Distinct conflicting aircraft pairs are counted once per episode. Flight safety status is permanent for scoring.

A live tier comprises 3 fixed seeds, each with 3,600 simulated seconds, 30-second decision ticks, and arrivals during the first 1,500 seconds. Sum safe landings across all three. A run passes the leaderboard safety gate only with zero separation losses, collisions, runway incidents and agent errors. Fuel exhaustion/unfinished flights earn zero but do not separately disqualify. Ties use smaller total spawn-to-landing time for all landed aircraft. Never compare raw safe-landing totals across different tiers.

Weather is a seed-specific per-airport speed reduction and runway-occupancy extension. In closure-enabled tiers, airport indexes 0, 3, 6, 9 close between seconds 900 and 1200. Current closure status and the public fixed schedule are available to the agent. Future arrival manifests are hidden from observations, although seeds/rules are public and can be reconstructed. Aircraft that remain airborne at the horizon receive no landing credit. Exact rules are in `config/benchmark.json`.

## Tracks and verification

- **Hosted competition:** server executes controls; complete all three seeds at one tier to publish. Public results include replay traces. No uploaded score is accepted as a hosted score.
- **Local full suite:** all eight tiers × three seeds, 24 complete traces, exact configuration fingerprint. `verify --official` reconstructs each episode and rejects changed scores, truncation, duplicate scenarios, noncanonical seeds and failed safety gates. PR submissions appear only in the separately generated `public/data/leaderboard.json` artifact, not the hosted board.
- **Practice:** chosen seed, one scenario, or stress multiplier through the engine constructor. Never eligible for either official track.

MCP and HTTP are two transports for the same service. Missing/invalid commands in the local runner fail the run. The lower-level engine records invalid actions as errors; the hosted service rejects invalid input before mutation. Hosted callers may choose 1–10 ticks per call; local agents are called every tick. Neither hosted nor local scores currently compare model cost, compute, or wall-clock latency. Report those separately when contributing results.

Names are self-reported, not authenticated teams. Server execution attests outcomes, not which model/policy produced the actions. Public seeds permit overfitting. A replay alone cannot prove that an agent used only observations or generalizes to unseen seeds. We make no hidden-evaluation claim. Future tournament protocols should add isolated policy execution, private seeds, compute budgets and authenticated submissions.

Rules are immutable within a version. Add harder tiers, weather models or controls only through reviewed configuration/version changes and migration notes. Never update the live challenge in response to one participant's score without freezing its old leaderboard.

## Overload tiers and historical versions

Tiers 6–8 add 576/768/960 flights across 12 airports. Their arrival windows are 900/600/450 seconds, fuel budgets 2100/1800/1500 seconds, and runway occupancy 90/105/120 seconds, before weather extensions. Tiers 7–8 have longer closures. These deliberately exceed runway capacity; missed flights earn zero while safety violations still fail admission. This is throughput optimization under overload, not an all-aircraft feasibility puzzle. Holding cells use three radial rings with vertically separated layers, avoiding impossible arrival distances caused solely by increasing aircraft count.

v0.1.0 is frozen in `config/releases/0.1.0.json`. Existing sessions and results retain their original rules and version. Replay verification selects the recorded version; leaderboard rows display it and must be compared within the same version and tier. Tiers 1–5 retain their physics in v0.2.0, but the full suite and configuration fingerprint changed.
